## My first project: a bag

When I asked **Claude** what projects to start with, it suggested a few, so I
chose to make a bag. I asked it to search for some good tutorials and from the
list I chose <a href="https://mellysews.com/sew-a-drawstring-bag/" rel="noopener
noreferrer" target="_blank"> Beginner Sewing Project: Learn to Sew a Drawstring
Bag from Melly Sews </a>

I mainly followed the video from the post.

The sizes were in inches so I needed to convert them to centimeters.

I used black thread on white material in order to see what I sewed.

I needed to obtain 2 pieces of material, `25 x 33 cm` each. Because the material
was already folded I was able to get exactly 2 identical pieces with a single
cut.

I used the following conversions:

> 1/4 inch = 0.6 cm
>
> 5/8 inch = 1.5 cm

**The default seam allowance is `5/8 inch`.**

As suggested in the video I created a guideline paper and also added an
additional guideline on the sewing machine (as it can be seen in the first
photo).

<ClickableImage
  className="post-image center big cursor-pointer"
  src="https://photos.alexprisacariu.dev/photos/post017_first_sewing_project/IMG_1763.webp"
  alt="Guideline paper"
/>

On each of the pieces, on the wrong side, I needed to fold the top part inwards
1/4 inch and then iron it. I tried to get it right using the guideline I
created, but it wasn't perfect. Later I figured out it didn't matter that it
wasn't 100% perfect, because that part goes inside the channels for the strings.

Then I needed to bring the 2 pieces together, with the good sides facing each
other (so the wrong sides are on the outside). **Tip for the future:** either
use pins or clips to make sure that the 2 pieces stay exactly in the same
position.

Then I needed to mark 2 inches from the top. In the tutorial she marked with a
pin. I marked with chalk. I think marking with chalk is better because the next
step is to sew the edges (but not the top one) and by marking with chalk I was
able to be more exact.

Next step, as I said already, was to sew the edges, but not the top one, with a
seam allowance of `5/8 inch`. I used the machine guidelines (plus the added
guidelines) to make sure I kept a straight line. Used the default machine
settings: **level 4 thread tension**, **2.5 stitch length**, **00 stitch mode**.

The result was quite good. It's true that it was a little tricky on the bottom
part and the left side (I started from the right side) to know when I reached
the "edge" of the seam allowance, so, as **a tip for the future**: mark the
spots where I need to stop.

The next step was to fold the "open" top left & right edges inwards twice (that
was the seam allowance, `5/8 inch`, of the sewn sides) and iron it.

After ironing it I needed to sew each side in the form of letter `U` so that the
bag won't unravel. This is best seen in the 2nd picture from below.

The second to last step was to fold the top "free" part outwards on each side
and then to sew the bottom part of it in order to form the channels for the
string.

Last step was to use pinking shears to cut the seam allowance in order to stop
the material from fraying. But I don't have pinking shears so I tried to use
zig-zag mode on the sewing machine (as I saw in other videos) to reach the same
result. I used the **04 stitch mode** with the default settings.

I did only one side (can be seen in the last picture) in order to test and the
result was OK. **Tip for the future**: trim the edges much shorter before doing
the zig-zag sewing.

The tutorial didn't say so, and I didn't do it even though I knew, but I should
have ironed all the seams after I did them to increase their resistance.
